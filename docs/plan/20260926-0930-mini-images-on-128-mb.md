## Context

The user asked (2026-09-26) for a mini build for x64 and arm64: no container engine, space
compressed to the extreme, and "the goal is that it still runs on 128 MB of flash". Measured at
`mica-build` `ebcc6e8` and `mica-core` `db3500b`, on the local `uefi-x64-dev` build.

### What forbids 128 MB today, independent of the image

Three reserves, written as constants:

- `mica-build:src/image/file-layout.ts` `checkCapacity`: SYSTEM must hold two deployments plus 128
  MiB. An ESP must hold two boot artifacts plus 64 MiB.
- `mica-core:crates/mica-deploy/src/deployments.rs:1055`: installing into SYSTEM requires 128 MiB
  free beyond the deployment.
- `mica-core:crates/mica-deploy/src/acquisition.rs:100`: every download into the DATA workspace
  requires 128 MiB free beyond the bytes it reserves.

These reserves alone are 320 MiB. A 128 MB part holds 122 MiB.

## Proposal

### The budget

For 128 MB (122 MiB), keeping A/B updates:

| Area | Size | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| GPT and alignment | 1 MiB | |
| ESP (FAT16) | 24 MiB | two UKIs of at most 10 MiB each, and systemd-boot |
| SYSTEM (ext4) | 60 MiB | two roots of at most 26 MB each, their verity data, ext4 overhead, a 4 MiB reserve |
| DATA | about 37 MiB at first boot; the rest of a larger part after growth | state, and the staging of one update |

The sizes are what the measurements below have to confirm, not a commitment. Three targets follow
from them:

- **The kernel at most 9 MB**, from 15.1 MB. The generic config builds the world in, because a
  verity root cannot load modules before the root is up. A mini kernel config keeps the floor
  (`common/kernel/mica-required.fragment`) and the virtual and generic UEFI drivers, and nothing
  else, compressed with xz.
- **The root at most 26 MB.** The root without podman is 34.0 MB even under xz, so about 8 MB has
  to go, in this order of measured size:
  - libc6's charset converters (gconv), which nothing in the root loads;
  - coreutils and part of util-linux, whose commands busybox already provides, where no unit or
    script needs the GNU behaviour;
  - `mica-apid`'s embedded UI assets, compressed or served from DATA;
  - mqtt, if the product does not need it (open question 2).
- **The reserves as policy, not constants.** They become facts of the board, in the signed boot
  policy's `board` section that `db3500b` introduced (and in `layout.tsv` for the build side). A
  board declares its SYSTEM, ESP and DATA staging reserves, and the device and the build both hold
  them. A generic board keeps 128 MiB; the mini boards declare a few MiB. On the DATA side the
  staging bound is what an update of this product actually needs. The delta transfer that
  `mica-core` is building (`bccab41`: an object assembled from chunks the device already holds)
  lowers it further.

### Phases

- **P0, the measurement** (`mica-build`, nothing shipped). A trial kernel with the mini config,
  built and packed into a UKI, and a trial root with the trims above, packed with xz. Their sizes
  decide the budget and which features fit. Measured, then reported, before anything below starts.
- **P1, `mica-core`: the reserves in the policy.** The `board` section gains the three reserves.
  `deployments.rs` and `acquisition.rs` read them in place of the constants. Tests: an install and
  a download refused at the declared reserve, and accepted just above it.
- **P2, `mica-build`: the mini boards.** Two new boards, `uefi-x64-mini` and `uefi-arm64-mini`, with:
  - the mini layout;
  - FAT16 in the ESP role;
  - the mini kernel config;
  - their reserves;
  - per-product squashfs compression (a product key, `xz` for the mini products);
  - the root trims as declared drops.

  Products `uefi-x64-mini` and `uefi-arm64-mini`: no `containers`. `checkCapacity` reads the
  declared reserves. Because they are new boards, this needs `db3500b` released and `mica-build`'s
  counterpart commit first (the running device refuses an unknown board name today), and the fleet
  plan (`20260926-0800-fleet-without-a-board-table`) for updates through the platform.
- **P3, the proof.** The lifecycle suite in QEMU on a 128 MB disk, x64 natively and arm64 under
  emulation:
  - first boot and DATA growth;
  - an update into the other slot;
  - a rollback.

  This runs as part of the products' release path, as the dev products' lifecycle stage does
  today.

## Risks

- **A root of 26 MB may not hold micad, apid and mqtt together.** P0 answers this with numbers. The
  product can drop mqtt, or DATA can host apid's UI, before the budget moves.
- **xz reads cost CPU** on every page fault of the root. It is measurable on arm64, and P3 records
  the boot time next to the dev product's.
- **A 9 MB kernel is a second config per architecture to maintain.** It is held by the same floor
  check (`common/kernel/floor-check.sh`) the generic kernels pass.
- **FAT16 below 32 MiB.** The EFI specification allows FAT12/16 on removable media, and on fixed
  disks firmware commonly reads them. QEMU's OVMF and the arm64 AAVMF do, which P3 exercises; real
  hardware is a per-board qualification.

## Scope

In:
- the three reserves (`mica-core`, `mica-build`);
- the mini kernel configs;
- the two mini boards and products;
- per-product compression;
- the root trims as declarations;
- the 128 MB lifecycle proof.

Out:
- RAM limits (not measured here);
- the FIT boards, whose layouts are their hardware's;
- any change to the existing boards and products.

## Open questions for the user

1. Keep A/B updates within 128 MB, as budgeted? The alternative is a single root slot with updates
   by reflash, which halves SYSTEM but loses rollback.
2. Which features must the mini products keep: micad and apid (management), mqtt? Each is measured
   in P0.
3. Boot time against size: xz everywhere, or zstd 22 where the difference is small?
